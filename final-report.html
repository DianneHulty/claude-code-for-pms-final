Final report · Claude Code for PMs · Rook Dispatch · 7 Nov 2026

Release 4.2 quietly pushed four reliable responders out of rotation.

4.2 gave responders 60 seconds to answer a ping instead of 90. They missed more. Our routing counts a missed ping exactly like a "no" and never lets it fade. So Meteor Mite, Farlight, The Undertow and Vesper slid to the back of the line within days, stopped being asked, and had no way back.

01 · What happened

One number changed. It switched on a rule that was already there.

4.2 didn't add a bad rule. It shortened the wait, and that exposed an old one: a missed ping costs as much as a turn-down, and the cost never wears off.

03 · The fix

A miss is not a no. Low standing fades back. Silence becomes a question.

Engineering could change one number this afternoon. Helen asked for something better: what a handler like Kip would notice, and what a quiet responder would feel. These are the four changes, each one something a person sees.

Phase 1

A miss is not a no

When a ping runs out, Mite's phone says the callout went to someone else, and that a miss doesn't count like a turn-down.

Responder notices
Phase 1

A bad stretch fades

Low standing drifts back toward normal over time, so one bad week doesn't follow a responder forever. This answers Wen's 2019 question.

Both notice
Phase 2

"Still on tonight?"

After 3 misses in a row, the phone asks. Yes puts Mite back in line. No answer marks Mite away, and Kip can see it.

Responder notices
Phase 2

Kip's card says why

"Asked 2 times since Wed, usually about 6. Why: 4 missed in a row." Kip can finally answer Mite's "is something broken?"

04 · Keeping it from happening again

4.2 would have failed all four of my skill's core checks.

The bug was in the code, but the real failure was in the decision: a number changed with no written problem, no owner for the intent, and nothing to measure. review-checklist checks for exactly those gaps before a brief goes out.
